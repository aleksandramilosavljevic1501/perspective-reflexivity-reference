Perspective on Reflexivity and Reference: Between Grammar and Discourse

Dates: 3–4 June 2027 (provisional)

Location: University of Graz, Austria

Invited Speaker: Elsi Kaiser (University of Southern California)

Workshop Description

The relationship between grammatical structure and discourse interpretation raises fundamental questions about how referential dependencies are established and interpreted. While traditional approaches to binding and anaphora have emphasized structural configurations and locality conditions, a substantial body of research has demonstrated that the distribution and interpretation of reflexive and referential expressions may also be sensitive to discourse-related factors, including prominence, accessibility, perspective, and information structure (Sells 1987; Zribi-Hertz 1989; König & Siemund 2000; Kaiser et al. 2009; Charnavel 2021; Lyu & Kaiser 2024).

This workshop aims to investigate the interaction between grammatical structure and discourse in the establishment and interpretation of reflexive and referential dependencies, with particular attention to the role of perspective. A central question is how structural, semantic, and discourse-related constraints interact in determining the distribution, interpretation, and processing of linguistic expressions. Discourse configurations may make alternative referents, antecedents, or perspective holders available, while grammatical mechanisms may constrain their accessibility, licensing, and interpretation. Conversely, grammatical expressions may themselves contribute to discourse organization by identifying referents, establishing perspective relations, or constraining contextually available interpretations.

These interactions raise broader theoretical questions about whether and how discourse-related information is represented in grammar. To what extent are reflexive and referential dependencies determined by dedicated grammatical features, representations, and structural configurations? Which aspects of their interpretation depend on discourse prominence, perspective-taking, information structure, or pragmatic inference? How do these different sources of information interact during real-time language processing? Furthermore, what can the morphological and interpretive connections between reflexives, pronouns, and intensifiers reveal about the relationship between grammatical representation and discourse interpretation?

By bringing together research from syntax, semantics, pragmatics, morphology, and psycholinguistics, the workshop seeks to advance our understanding of how grammatical structure and discourse interact in shaping reflexivity, reference, and perspective-sensitive interpretation. Contributions employing formal-theoretical, experimental, corpus-based, descriptive, and crosslinguistic approaches are welcome, whether they investigate individual phenomena or explore connections between them.

Call for Papers

We invite submissions for presentations addressing the relationship between grammatical structure, discourse, reflexivity, reference, and perspective.

Topics of Interest

We welcome contributions addressing, but not limited to, the following topics:

  • Reflexivity and binding: Local and long-distance reflexivity, exempt anaphora, structural and nonstructural binding, reflexivization, and argument structure.
  • Logophoricity and perspective: Logophoric pronouns and anaphors, perspective holders, perspective shifting, empathy, attitude reports, indexicality, and de se interpretation.
  • Reference and coreference: Pronominal interpretation, antecedent accessibility, discourse prominence, referential dependencies, and the interaction between grammatical features and discourse referents.
  • Discourse and grammatical dependencies: Information structure, contrast, contextual alternatives, and the role of discourse configurations in licensing and interpreting reflexive and referential expressions.
  • Reflexivity, intensification, and morphological multifunctionality: Formal and interpretive connections between reflexives, pronouns, and intensifiers, including emphatic and contrastive effects.
  • Experimental and processing perspectives: The interaction of structural, semantic, and discourse-related constraints in anaphora resolution, perspective-taking, and referential interpretation.
  • Crosslinguistic variation and grammatical representation: Variation in reflexive, logophoric, and referential systems and its implications for theories of grammar and discourse.

Contributions may focus on reflexivity, reference, perspective, or the connections between them. The workshop welcomes different theoretical perspectives, with particular interest in research that clarifies the respective contributions of grammatical structure and discourse to linguistic interpretation.
